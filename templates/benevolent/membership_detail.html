Account adjustments — penalties, waivers, write-offs

None of these is money, and none of them posts an accounting entry. A penalty charged is not income — nobody has paid it, and they may never; it becomes income on the day it is actually paid, as an ordinary receipt. A waiver is not an expense — no money left the church; it simply stopped asking. What they change is one number: what this member owes.

Refunds — real money, leaving the bank

A refund is not the same as reversing a receipt. A receipt that should never have existed — wrong member, duplicate, bounced — is reversed: the church never had that money. A receipt that was correct, where money is genuinely handed back, is refunded: the church really received it and is really paying it out, and both facts belong in the cash book. So a refund is an ordinary payment voucher, and it clears the usual approval like any other.
